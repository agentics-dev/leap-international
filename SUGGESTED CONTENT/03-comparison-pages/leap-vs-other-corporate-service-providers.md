Across hundreds of founder conversations, the same three frustrations come up with conventional providers

1. **You can't see the price before you talk to sales.** Comparing providers means three quotation rounds
   and a week of emails. Leap publishes every package and fee on the [pricing page](/pages/pricing.html).
2. **You never meet the person doing the work.** Filings pass through layers of account management. At Leap

3. **Small clients get small attention.** In a large group, an SME account sits at the bottom of the queue.
   Leap only serves founders and SMEs — that is the whole business, not the bottom tier.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Leap International a good alternative to large corporate service providers?
For Hong Kong-focused founders and SMEs, yes — the statutory scope (incorporation, company secretary,
accounting, audit) is identical, delivered with published pricing and a founder-led team. If you need
multi-jurisdiction coverage or fund services, a larger group is the right call.

### Is Leap cheaper than other corporate service providers?
Leap publishes its prices; many providers quote case by case. For a standard Hong Kong SME scope, our
published packages (from HK$[VERIFY]) are typically below large-group quotes, mainly because you are not
paying for global infrastructure you don't use. Always compare identical scopes.

### Can I switch to Leap from another provider?
Yes. Switching is routine: the change of company secretary/registered office is filed with the Companies
Registry and statutory records are handed over. Leap handles the transition paperwork —
[contact us](/pages/contact.html) for a switch-over quote.

### What can larger providers do that Leap does not?
Fund administration, trust services, listed-company compliance and multi-country entity management are
beyond a Hong Kong-focused boutique's scope. If you need those, we will tell you so directly.

### Does provider size affect filing quality?
No. Every provider files against the same Companies Registry and IRD requirements. Size changes the service
model, the pricing approach and who you speak to — not the statutory work itself.
