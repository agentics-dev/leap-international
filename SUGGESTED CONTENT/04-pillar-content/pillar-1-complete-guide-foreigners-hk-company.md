## 1. Why choose Hong Kong? Advantages and honest challenges for foreign founders

**The advantages, factually:**

- **Simple, fast incorporation.** No residency requirements, no minimum capital, online filing, and
  certificates in days rather than weeks.
- **Low, simple taxation.** Two-tier profits tax — 8.25% on the first HK$2 million of assessable profits,
  16.5% above. No VAT, GST, sales tax, capital gains tax, or dividend withholding tax (source: ird.gov.hk).
- **Territorial tax system.** Generally, only profits sourced in Hong Kong are taxable — offshore profits
  can be claimed as non-taxable with proper evidence.
- **A common-law jurisdiction with English contracts.** Predictable courts, enforceable agreements, and
  company law in English.
- **Gateway positioning.** Direct access to Mainland China and Asia markets, a free port, and one of the
  world's most established banking systems.

**The honest challenges:**

- **Banking is the hard part.** Account opening due diligence is strict for foreign-owned companies;
  timelines of [VERIFY: 2–6] weeks are normal. Plan for it (Section 5).
- **Compliance is not optional.** Annual returns, audits and tax filings apply to every company — budget
  for them from day one (Section 4).
- **Substance matters.** For offshore tax claims and some bank applications, where you actually manage the
  company matters. Get advice before claiming offshore status.

**The process:**

1. **Name check and structure.** Confirm the name is available and decide the share structure
   (HK$10,000 / 10,000 ordinary shares is the common default).
2. **Prepare the incorporation documents.** Form NNC1 (incorporation form for a company limited by shares),
   Articles of Association, and the incorporation fee of HK$1,720. A provider prepares and files these.
3. **File via e-Registry.** Certificates (Certificate of Incorporation + Business Registration Certificate)
   typically issue within [VERIFY: 1–3] business days. The one-stop process registers you with the Inland
   Revenue Department at the same time.
4. **Open the bank account.** Start immediately in parallel — it is the longest step (Section 5).
5. **Set up compliance from day one.** Accounting records from the first transaction, a calendar for the
   annual return, and an audit engagement before year-end (Section 4).

## 3. Visas and residency: owning versus living in Hong Kong

**Owning needs no visa.** Any nationality can own and direct a Hong Kong company entirely from abroad.

**Living and working in Hong Kong does.** The main routes for founders:

- **Investment as Entrepreneurs visa (GEP).** For founders relocating to run their Hong Kong business.
  No fixed minimum investment; the Immigration Department assesses the business plan, financial resources
  and contribution to the local economy case by case. Operating substance — real office, real transactions,
  ideally local hiring — is what successful applications demonstrate. [VERIFY current criteria at immd.gov.hk]
- **Top Talent Pass Scheme (TTPS).** For individuals meeting high-income or top-university criteria; allows
  you to live in Hong Kong and then build a business. [VERIFY current criteria]
- **Quality Migrant Admission Scheme (QMAS).** Points-based route not tied to a specific business.

**Practical sequence most founders use:** incorporate first (remote), build trading history and substance,
then apply for the entrepreneur visa with real numbers rather than projections.

## 4. Operating legally: company secretary, accounting and annual compliance

Every Hong Kong company, however small, carries the same annual obligations:

| Obligation | Deadline | What happens if you miss it |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual return (NAR1)** | Within 42 days of each incorporation anniversary | Escalating late fees; possible prosecution |
| **Audited financial statements** | Every financial year (audit by a HK CPA) | Cannot validly file profits tax; penalties |
| **Profits tax return** | Issued by IRD, usually first issued ~18 months after incorporation, then annually | Estimated assessments and penalties |
| **Business Registration renewal** | Annually | Penalties; company can be struck off for persistent default |
| **Employer's returns (if staff)** | Annually (BIR56A/IR56 series) | Penalties |

**The company secretary** keeps the statutory registers, files the NAR1, processes share/director changes
and watches these deadlines. It is legally mandatory and cannot be the sole director of a single-member
company. [Leap's corporate secretary service](/pages/corporate-secretary.html) covers the full scope.

**Accounting and audit** go together: keep books from day one ([bookkeeping](/pages/bookkeeping.html) and
[accounting](/pages/accounting.html) plans from HK$[VERIFY: 3,500]/year), then the annual
[audit and profits tax filing](/pages/audit-tax-filing.html) closes the year.

## 7. Frequently asked questions

### Can foreigners start a company in Hong Kong without living there?
Yes — there is no residency or nationality requirement for directors or shareholders, and incorporation is
fully remote via e-Registry. You need a Hong Kong registered office and company secretary, which service
providers supply.

### How fast can I be operational?
Incorporation: [VERIFY: 1–3] business days. Bank account: [VERIFY: 2–6] weeks. Most founders are trading
within [VERIFY: 4–8] weeks of starting.

### Do I need a Hong Kong partner or local shareholder?
No. 100% foreign ownership is standard and requires no local participation of any kind.

### What visa do I need if I want to move to Hong Kong to run my company?
The Investment as Entrepreneurs visa (GEP) is the standard founder route; TTPS and QMAS are alternatives
depending on your profile. Ownership alone never requires a visa.

### What are the annual compliance obligations?
Annual return (NAR1) within 42 days of the incorporation anniversary, annual audited accounts by a HK CPA,
annual profits tax return, and Business Registration renewal. Leap bundles all of these into one
[compliance package](/pages/corporate-secretary.html).

### Is a Hong Kong company good for e-commerce or online businesses?
Yes — no VAT/GST, simple profits tax, and multi-currency banking make it a common base for cross-border
e-commerce. The main care-point is where profits are sourced if you plan an offshore claim; get advice
specific to your transaction flows.

### Can I close or pause the company if plans change?
Yes. A solvent company can be deregistered once filings and taxes are settled; dormant status is possible
for pauses. Your corporate secretary handles the filings either way.
