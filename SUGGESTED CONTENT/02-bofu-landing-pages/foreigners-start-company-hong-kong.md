You do **not** need: Hong Kong residency, a Hong Kong partner, a minimum share capital (HK$1 is enough),
a local bank account before incorporation, or a physical office lease.

## Step-by-step: from decision to incorporation

1. **Choose a company name** — English, Chinese, or both; checked against the Companies Registry index.
   Names cannot duplicate an existing registered name.
2. **Prepare the particulars** — at least one shareholder and one director (same person can hold both;
   any nationality; corporate shareholders allowed), plus passport copies and proof of address for each.
3. **File incorporation (Form NNC1)** — submitted to the Companies Registry with the Articles of
   Association. Government fees: HK$1,720 incorporation fee + first-year Business Registration fee
   (HK$[VERIFY: 2,200] — confirm at ird.gov.hk).
4. **Receive your certificates** — Certificate of Incorporation and Business Registration Certificate,
   typically within [VERIFY: 1–3] business days via e-Registry.
5. **Open a bank account** — see below.
6. **Stay compliant** — annual return (NAR1) within 42 days of each incorporation anniversary, annual
   audit, and profits tax return. Leap covers all three: [annual return filing](/pages/annual-return-filing.html),
   [accounting](/pages/accounting.html), [audit & tax filing](/pages/audit-tax-filing.html).

## The bank account question (the part most guides skip)

Incorporation is fast; banking is the slower step. Traditional Hong Kong banks run full customer due

## Why foreigners choose Hong Kong (the short, factual version)

- **Two-tier profits tax:** 8.25% on the first HK$2 million of assessable profits, 16.5% above that (IRD).
- **Territorial taxation:** generally only Hong Kong-sourced profits are taxed.
- **No VAT/GST, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax on dividends.**
- **Free port** with no customs duties on general imports/exports.
- **Common-law legal system** and contracts enforceable in English.

## Common mistakes foreign founders make

1. **Forgetting the company secretary is mandatory** — every Hong Kong company must have one from day one;
   it cannot be the sole director if the sole director is the only member.
2. **Budgeting only for setup** — annual compliance (return, audit, tax filing) is not optional; price it in.
3. **Assuming the bank account is automatic** — start banking documents in parallel with incorporation.
4. **Letting the Business Registration lapse** — it renews annually, separately from the annual return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a foreigner be the sole director and sole shareholder of a Hong Kong company?
Yes. One person of any nationality can be both the only director and the only shareholder. No Hong Kong
resident director is required.

### Do I need a visa to own a Hong Kong company?
No visa is required to *own* a company. You only need a visa (e.g. the Investment as Entrepreneurs visa)
if you plan to *live and work* in Hong Kong. See our [visa & residency pillar guide](../04-pillar-content/pillar-1-complete-guide-foreigners-hk-company.md) for the options.

### How long does the whole process take, end to end?
Incorporation: [VERIFY: 1–3] business days. Bank account: [VERIFY: 2–6] weeks. Most founders are fully
operational within [VERIFY: 4–8] weeks.

### Is there a minimum capital requirement?
No statutory minimum. Most companies start with HK$1–HK$10,000 in share capital; HK$10,000 is common
because it keeps future share transfers simple.

### Can I run the company entirely from my home country?
Yes. Directors can manage a Hong Kong company from anywhere. The local obligations (secretary, registered
office, filings, audit) are handled in Hong Kong on your behalf.
